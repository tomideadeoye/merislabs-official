The ESG Ecosystem

Environment, Social, and Governance criteria have evolved into a crucial method for assessing long-term value creation.

🌿 Environmental

Performance as a steward of the natural world. Climate strategy, carbon management, resource efficiency, and biodiversity protection.

🤝 Social

Stakeholder relationship management. Labour standards, health & safety, diversity, human rights, and community engagement.

⚖️ Governance

Leadership and operational structure. Board independence, audit transparency, shareholder rights, and anti-corruption policies.

Structural Barriers

The gap is caused by a lopsided relationship between demand-side readiness and supply-side requirements.

Demand Side

  • Limited Awareness

    Knowledge gap regarding ESG metrics and climate risk disclosures.

  • Poor Reporting Capacity

    Only 9% of SMEs operate formal sustainability reporting programs.

  • Survival Focus

    Short-term cash flow needs supersede long-term green planning.

Supply Side

  • The Information Gap

    Traditional credit metrics are insufficient for modern ESG due diligence.

  • Risk Perception

    Lack of data heightens perceived exposure to environmental and social shocks.

  • Regulatory Disparity

    Lopsided compliance burdens between financiers and borrowers.

The Way Forward

Transitioning to a greener and more inclusive Nigerian economy by 2060.

The disconnect between financier expectations and SME realities requires coordinated legislative action. Mandating disclosure standards aligned with international best practices is no longer optional—it is vital for SME survival in a global economy that prioritizes sustainability.

Key Action Points

  • Mandatory legislative action for enabling environments.
  • Digital transformation for automated ESG reporting.
  • Phased implementation of ISSB (IFRS S1 & S2) standards.
  • Developing SME-specific ESG products.
